## 1. Measured non-use of open ML artifacts (Hugging Face Hub)

### Takeaway
On the Hugging Face Hub, roughly 70% of models and datasets had zero downloads in an October 2023 snapshot, under 1% of models accounted for 80% of downloads, and about two thirds had no licence; a 2025 to 2026 analysis by Hugging Face co-authors still finds that about half of models have fewer than 200 total downloads and 200 models (about 0.01%) take about half of all downloads. Non-use is the norm and is measurable from public metadata, but "downloads" is a noisy proxy.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Osborne, Ding and Kirk (2024, J. Computational Social Science; arXiv 2405.13058). Sample: 348,181 model, 65,761 dataset and 156,642 space repositories collected via the HF Hub API in October 2023. Findings: 71% of models and 70% of datasets have 0 downloads; 91% of models and 88% of datasets have 0 likes; 84% of models, 91% of datasets and 96% of spaces have 0 discussions. — [arXiv 2405.13058 v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13058v1); journal version [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42001-024-00300-8) [S]
- **[V]** Same study, concentration: under 1% of models account for 80% of downloads and 1% account for 99%; under 1% of models account for 80% of likes; 30% of models account for 80% of commits. — [arXiv 2405.13058 v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13058v1)
- **[V]** Same study, collaboration: 87% of model repositories have a single contributor; 89% of developers in the model collaboration network are isolates. — [arXiv 2405.13058 v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13058v1)
- **[V]** Same study, licensing: 65% of model repositories and 72% of dataset repositories have no licence (licence analysis over 339,502 repositories). Among licensed models: Apache-2.0 37%, MIT 17%, OpenRAIL 14%, CreativeML OpenRAIL-M 10%. — [arXiv 2405.13058 v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13058v1)
- **[V]** Same study, downstream use inside the Hub: only 3 models are used in 1,000 or more Spaces; at least 50% of models that are used in Spaces are used in only one Space (mean 6.68, median 1). — [arXiv 2405.13058 v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13058v1)
- **[V]** Castaño, Martínez-Fernández, Franch and Bogner (arXiv 2311.13380, v2 Feb 2024; MSR 2024). Sample: more than 380,000 HF models and 2,760,224 commits, collected 6 November 2023. Findings: commits per model mean 7.16, median 3; 71% of models have fewer than 5 commits; authors per model mean 1.18, median 1; commit types 89.3% perfective, 6.1% adaptive, 2.46% corrective. A k-means (k=2) maintenance classifier labels 62,818 models (about 16.4%) "high maintenance" and 319,477 (about 83.5%) "low maintenance" (the paper's text reverses the two percentages in one place; the counts imply this reading). — [arXiv 2311.13380 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.13380)
- **[V, derived]** Model cards: Castaño et al. used 87,775 published model cards for topic analysis out of about 380,000 models, i.e. roughly 23% had a card usable for analysis. The paper does not state this percentage itself. — [arXiv 2311.13380 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.13380)
- **[V]** Longpre, Akiki, Lund, Kulkarni, Chen, Solaiman, Ghosh, Jernite, Kaffee, "Economies of Open Intelligence" (arXiv 2512.03073, 27 Nov 2025). Sample: HF download logs from June 2020 to mid-2025 (text says August 2025, appendix says July 2025), 2.2B downloads, 1.88M models on the Hub. Their "population sample" keeps 851,000 models and covers 97.6% of downloads; models with fewer than 200 total downloads were excluded. The "head sample" of 2,875 models (top 200 per week across 265 weeks) accounts for 49.6% of all downloads. — [arXiv 2512.03073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03073v1)
- **[V, derived]** From the above: about 1.03M of 1.88M models (about 55%) have fewer than 200 lifetime downloads and together account for only about 2.4% of downloads. Derived by subtraction; not a figure the paper states. — [arXiv 2512.03073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03073v1)
- **[V]** Hugging Face, "State of Open Source on Hugging Face: Spring 2026" (17 March 2026): more than 2 million public models, over 500,000 public datasets, 13 million users in 2025; "roughly half of models have fewer than 200 total downloads"; the top 200 most-downloaded models (about 0.01%) account for 49.6% of downloads; average engagement with an open model lasts about 6 weeks. No zero-download figure is given. — [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/huggingface/state-of-os-hf-spring-2026)
- **[V]** Longpre et al. on download inflation: they apply a "rolling window filter" that counts downloads only within one year of a model's creation to reduce inflation from automated processes, citing vLLM's CI/CD repeatedly downloading OPT-125M. Intermediary re-uploaders (lmstudio-community, comfy, mlx-community) account for over 22% of recent downloads. — [arXiv 2512.03073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03073v1)
- **[V]** How HF counts model downloads (official docs): counted server-side on requests to "query files" (default `config.json`, also `config.yaml`, `hyperparams.yaml`, `params.json`, `meta.yaml`; libraries can override); "Every HTTP request to these files, including GET and HEAD, will be counted as a download." The docs describe no deduplication by user/IP and no bot or CI filtering; excluding CI/CD requires the owner-only Publisher Analytics granular logs. — [HF Hub docs: models-download-stats](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/models-download-stats)
- **[V]** Hugging Face's own "Data for Research" README warns that download counts are "useful but imperfect", best for global and temporal trends and relative comparisons rather than fine-grained rankings. — [HF Data for Research README](https://hf-data-for-research-readme.static.hf.space/README.md)
- **[V]** Jiang et al., PeaTMOSS (MSR 2024; arXiv 2310.03620): metadata for 281,638 PTM packages (Hugging Face and PyTorch Hub), 15,250 full snapshots (HF packages with over 10K downloads plus all PyTorch Hub), and 44,337 links from PTMs to downstream GitHub repositories (abstract: 27,270 repos; another passage says 28,575; the source is internally inconsistent). GitHub projects up to 10 July 2023. The paper does not report the share of models with no downstream GitHub use. Access: SQLite metadata (7.12 GB) and full set (48.2 TB) via Globus at Purdue. — [arXiv 2310.03620](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03620)
- **[V, derived]** PeaTMOSS implies an upper bound on visible reuse: 15,250 packages out of 281,638 (about 5.4%) cleared the "over 10K downloads or PyTorch Hub" bar for a full snapshot. Derived; the paper frames this as a storage decision, not an adoption statistic. — [arXiv 2310.03620](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03620)

### Inferences
- The zero-download share (about 70% in Oct 2023) and the "under 200 lifetime downloads" share (about 50 to 55% in 2025) are not the same metric and should not be presented as a trend. Both support the same qualitative claim: most Hub artifacts show no measurable uptake.
- Because HEAD requests and CI count as downloads and there is no deduplication, a small non-zero download count is weak evidence of use; conversely zero downloads in the 30-day window is fairly strong evidence of no current use via the Hub (but not of no use via mirrors, GGUF re-uploads, or local copies).
- Re-uploaders taking over 22% of recent downloads means adoption of a model is often recorded under a different repository than the original. A tracker needs a "derivative/mirror of" link or it will mislabel widely used originals as unused.
- Most Hub repositories are personal fine-tunes and checkpoints, not "tools". The new dataset should not use the Hub-wide base rate as its headline number for "AI tools going unused"; it needs an inclusion criterion (e.g. has an associated paper, a stated intended use, or a public-interest claim).

### Gaps
- Share of HF models with no model card: no study fetched states a direct percentage. [U] Background: Liang et al., "What's documented in AI? Systematic analysis of 32K AI model cards" (Nature Machine Intelligence 2024) reported that a minority of models have cards but that those models account for the vast majority of downloads; exact figures not verified here.
- Jiang et al., "An Empirical Study of Pre-Trained Model Reuse in the Hugging Face Deep Learning Model Registry" (ICSE 2023): not fetched. [U] It is primarily an interview study (12 practitioners) plus a registry analysis of reuse challenges and supply-chain risks; no non-use rate recalled. Not found: a quantified unused share from this paper.
- No verified post-2023 measurement of the zero-download share. Could be recomputed cheaply from the `cfahlgren1/hub-stats` daily Parquet snapshot.
- No study found measuring non-use of HF datasets beyond Osborne et al.'s 70% zero-download figure.

## 2. Measured abandonment of open-source and research software; code release rates; Papers with Code status

### Takeaway
Abandonment is common and well measured: about 64% of 18,247 scientific GitHub repositories were inactive (no commit for 6 months) by 2023, fewer than half of sampled package repositories survive five years, and 28% of omics tool URLs from 2005 to 2017 papers were dead with 28% of sampled tools not installable at all. Most AI papers historically did not release code (20.5% across ten top AI venues in 2010 to 2019, though NeurIPS reached about 65% by 2021). Papers with Code was shut down by Meta in July 2025; only archived dumps remain.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Coelho and Valente, "Why Modern Open Source Projects Fail" (ESEC/FSE 2017). Sample: top 5,000 GitHub repositories by stars, September 2016. 628 (13%) had no commits in the last year; 207 READMEs (4%) contained "deprecated/unmaintained"-type terms, of which 76 (37%) were true positives on manual review. 104 projects were confirmed as failed; top reasons: usurped by competitor (27), obsolete (20), lack of time (18), lack of interest (18), outdated technologies (14). — [arXiv 1707.02327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02327)
- **[V]** Avelino, Constantinou, Valente and Serebrenik, "On the abandonment and survival of open source projects" (ESEM 2019; arXiv 1906.08058). Sample: 1,932 popular GitHub projects (top-starred in six languages). 315 projects (16%) faced at least one "truck factor developer detachment" (all core developers gone); 128 of those (41%) survived by attracting new core developers; 59% of detachments happened in the first two years; 66% occurred in projects with truck factor 1. A developer counts as having abandoned a project when their last commit is at least one year before the latest repository commit (1 year chosen after testing 3 months to 2 years). — [arXiv 1906.08058](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08058)
- **[V]** Ait, Izquierdo and Cabot, "An Empirical Study on the Survival Rate of GitHub Projects" (MSR 2022). Sample: 1,127 repositories from four ecosystems (NPM, R, WordPress plugins, Laravel), all created in 2016. Probability of surviving longer than five years is "less than 50%"; "a significant number of projects die in the first year". Exact inactivity definition not shown on the fetched abstract page. — [MSR 2022 page](https://conf.researchr.org/details/msr-2022/msr-2022-technical-papers/16/An-Empirical-Study-on-the-Survival-Rate-of-GitHub-Projects)
- **[V]** "Scientific Open-Source Software Is Less Likely to Become Abandoned Than One Might Think! Lessons from Curating a Catalog of Maintained Scientific Software" (arXiv 2504.18971, 2025; SciCat dataset). Sample: 18,247 scientific repositories from World of Code (data to spring 2023, last commit after November 2018 required), matched to 36,494 non-scientific projects. Definition: no commits in the six months before end of data collection = inactive (sensitivity checks up to 24 months did not change results). Restricted mean survival over 15 years: 6.44 years overall; application-layer (publication-specific) code 3.94 years, domain-specific 6.52, infrastructure 7.44; Computer Science shortest at 5.74 years. Scientific software had a hazard ratio of 0.92 versus matched non-scientific projects (about 8% lower abandonment risk). Academic participation hazard ratio 1.08; government participation 0.86; having downstream projects 0.78. — [arXiv 2504.18971](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18971)
- **[V, derived]** In the same study, 11,631 of 18,244 scientific projects in the Cox model were inactive, about 64%. Derived from reported counts. — [arXiv 2504.18971](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18971)
- **[V]** Mangul et al., "Challenges and recommendations to improve the installability and archival stability of omics computational tools" (PLOS Biology 2019). Sample: 36,702 software-resource URLs from omics papers published 2005 to 2017, and 98 randomly selected tools for install testing. "Almost 28% of all resources are currently not accessible through URLs published in the paper" (15.9% broken, 11.9% timed out). 51% of tools were "easy to install" (15 minutes or less); 28% "failed to be installed at all" within a 2-hour limit; 57.1% failed the automatic installation test. Installed tools had significantly more citations than non-installable ones (Mann-Whitney p = 0.032). — [PMC6605654](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6605654)
- **[V]** Code release rates by venue (arXiv 2308.10008, "What is the Impact of Releasing Code with Publications? Statistics from the Machine Learning, Robotics, and Control Communities", data collected August 2022). Share of papers with open-source code: NeurIPS 27.6% (2016), 40.5% (2017), 49.1% (2018), 69.7% (2019), 63.1% (2020), 65.5% (2021); ICRA 1.7% (2016) to 4.9% (2021); CDC 0.6% (2016) to 2.6% (2021). Authors report a maximum quantified error of 4.7%. Author names not confirmed in the fetch. — [arXiv 2308.10008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10008)
- **[V]** "Automatic Analysis of Available Source Code of Top Artificial Intelligence Conference Papers" (arXiv 2209.14155, 2022): across regular papers at 10 top AI conferences from 2010 to 2019, 20.5% had available source code, and 8.1% of the identified repositories were no longer accessible. — [arXiv 2209.14155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14155v1)
- **[V, secondary]** Papers with Code status: Meta shut the site down in July 2025 (observed window 24 to 25 July 2025); users are pushed toward Hugging Face; the old `paperswithcode.com/api/v1` endpoints are not live; archived JSON snapshots of papers, datasets, methods and evaluations remain in the GitHub repository `paperswithcode/paperswithcode-data`. Source is CodeSOTA, a commercial would-be successor, so treat framing with care. — [CodeSOTA shutdown record](https://www.codesota.com/papers-with-code/shutdown)
- **[V, secondary]** Successors named by that source: Hugging Face Papers (huggingface.co/papers) as a discovery feed, Semantic Scholar API for metadata, and CodeSOTA for leaderboards. No drop-in replacement exists. — [CodeSOTA shutdown record](https://www.codesota.com/papers-with-code/shutdown)
- **[S]** A later headline, "Hugging Face Revives PapersWithCode Benchmark Tracker", appeared in search results; content not read, date and scope unconfirmed. — [aiweekly.co listing](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/hugging-face-revives-paperswithcode-benchmark-tracker)
- **[V]** The OECD.AI catalogue FAQ still describes metrics as coming "from Papers with Code", showing that downstream trackers depended on it. — [OECD.AI tools FAQ](https://oecd.ai/en/tools-faq)
- **[V, secondary]** A May 2026 analysis of CHAOSS metrics cites a "Weekend at Bernie's" analysis that placed "a meaningful share of the most-depended-on packages across sixteen registries in a dead-or-unresponsive bucket" (no number given), and argues that activity-count metrics are now inflated by AI-generated issues and pull requests. — [nesbitt.io, CHAOSS metrics in 2026](https://nesbitt.io/2026/05/27/chaoss-metrics-in-2026.html)

### Inferences
- Thresholds in the literature converge on three inactivity cut-offs: 6 months (SciCat), 12 months (Coelho and Valente; Avelino et al.), with 24 months as a conservative sensitivity check. The OECD catalogue independently removes tools not updated within two years. A new dataset can cite these directly instead of inventing a threshold.
- Inactivity is not failure: Coelho and Valente found only 104 confirmed failures among 618 inactive or deprecated-looking top projects (about 17%, derived), because many inactive projects are simply finished. The new dataset should separate "inactive" from "unused" and allow a "complete/stable" status.
- Publication-specific research code has the shortest life (about 3.9 years restricted mean survival), which is exactly the category "AI tool published with a paper" falls into.
- Code-release rates differ by an order of magnitude across fields (NeurIPS about 65% versus control and robotics under 6% in 2021), so "published but unused" is a problem mainly where code is released at all; in other fields the problem is non-release.

### Gaps
- URSSI and Software Sustainability Institute: no quantified abandonment or non-use statistic found in this session. Not found.
- Papers with Code's own "share of papers with code" trend statistic: not retrievable (site is down); a `ml.paperswithcode.com/trends` URL appeared in search but was not fetched. [U] Background: the figure commonly quoted from PwC was roughly a quarter to a third of ML arXiv papers having linked code; unverified.
- The exact redirect target of paperswithcode.com today was not independently confirmed (only CodeSOTA's description). [U] Background: it redirected to Hugging Face's "Trending Papers" page.
- Ait et al.'s precise "dead" definition and first-year death percentage were not on the fetched page.
- URL-decay studies beyond Mangul et al. (e.g. general link rot in scholarly papers) were not researched.
- Stanford AI Index figures on code availability were not retrieved.

## 3. The research-to-deployment gap in applied ML

### Takeaway
The best-sourced deployment-gap numbers come from clinical AI: none of 62 rigorously reviewed COVID-19 imaging models was clinically usable (Roberts et al. 2021), and of 500+ FDA-cleared AI devices only two had more than 10,000 reimbursed claims over about four years (Wu et al., NEJM AI 2023). Widely repeated industry statistics ("87% of projects never reach production") are weakly sourced and should not be used as evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **[V, institutional press release for the paper]** Roberts et al., "Common pitfalls and recommendations for using machine learning to detect and prognosticate for COVID-19 using chest radiographs and CT scans" (Nature Machine Intelligence, March 2021): 2,212 studies identified (published 1 January to 3 October 2020), 415 passed initial screening, 62 passed quality screening; none of the 62 models was suitable for clinical use, owing to methodological flaws and/or underlying biases. — [University of Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/machine-learning-models-for-diagnosing-covid-19-are-not-yet-suitable-for-clinical-use)
- **[V, secondary]** Wu et al., NEJM AI (2023), claims analysis: about 11 billion insurance claims (IQVIA PharMetrics Plus for MedTech, 1 January 2018 to 1 June 2023); 16 medical-AI procedures identified via 32 CPT codes; "just a handful made up most claims". — [2 Minute Medicine summary](https://www.2minutemedicine.com/rapid-growth-of-medical-artificial-intelligence-technology-usage-identified-from-insurance-claims-analysis-yet-major-barriers-to-widespread-adoption-remain/)
- **[V, secondary]** Same study as reported by Health Management, Policy and Innovation (Nov 2024): of the 500+ FDA-cleared AI devices, only two had "over 10,000 CPT claims reimbursed in a four-year period" (coronary artery disease assessment and diabetic retinopathy diagnosis). The same article cites El Fassi et al. (Nature Medicine 2024) that "almost half of authorized tools were not clinically validated". — [HMPI](https://hmpi.org/2024/11/17/why-ai-is-good-for-our-health-but-may-hurt-our-wallets/)
- **[V]** DPG registry evidence for public-interest AI: a 2026 UNU research report (Stinckwich et al., commissioned by the Asian Development Bank) states the DPG registry held 241 verified DPGs at the beginning of 2026, the "Open AI System" category had only a small number of submissions under tentative review in late 2025, and the DPGA Secretariat had not identified any AI system that fully meets the DPG Standard as of 2025. — [UNU, AI Systems as Digital Public Goods](https://unu.edu/sites/default/files/2026-06/AI_Systems_as_Digital_Public_Goods.pdf)
- **[S, number is U]** Gartner press release, 7 May 2024, "Gartner Survey Finds Generative AI Is Now the Most Frequently Deployed AI Solution in Organizations". The fetch failed (site error). [U] Background recollection: 644 respondents (US, Germany, UK; Q4 2023); "on average, only 48% of AI projects make it into production", taking about 8 months from prototype to production. Self-reported by organisations already doing AI, enterprise projects not open-source tools. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-05-07-gartner-survey-finds-generative-ai-is-now-the-most-frequently-deployed-ai-solution-in-organizations)
- **[S]** A June 2025 Gartner release is titled "Gartner Survey Finds 45% of Organizations With High AI Maturity Keep AI Projects Operational for at Least Three Years"; content not read. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-30-gartner-survey-finds-forty-five-percent-of-organizations-with-high-artificial-intelligence-maturity-keep-artificial-intelligence-projects-operational-for-at-least-three-years)
- **[S]** The "87% / 85% / 90% / 95% of AI projects never reach production" family of statistics appears across vendor blogs and Medium posts in search results, with no primary survey attached in the listings. — example listing: [Medium/TDS](https://medium.com/data-science/why-90-percent-of-all-machine-learning-models-never-make-it-into-production-ce7e250d5a4a)
- **[V]** Lavin et al., "Technology readiness levels for machine learning systems" (Nature Communications 13, 6039, 2022) define levels 0 First principles, 1 Goal-oriented research, 2 Proof of principle, 3 System development, 4 Proof of concept, 5 Machine learning "capability", 6 Application development, 7 Integrations, 8 Mission-ready, 9 Deployment. Useful as an ordinal "how far did it get" field. — [PMC9585100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9585100)

### Inferences
- Two different gaps are being measured and should be kept apart in the new dataset: (a) a quality gap (models that could not be used safely, as in Roberts et al.), and (b) an adoption gap (cleared or working tools that are not taken up, as in Wu et al.). "Published but unused" spans both, and the reasons differ.
- Enterprise "share reaching production" surveys measure internal corporate projects, not open-source releases. They do not support a claim about open tools going unused.
- For clinical AI, reimbursement claims are a rare example of an external, auditable usage signal. Analogous auditable signals for open tools are scarce, which is the core measurement problem the new dataset must address.

[U] Background recollection, not verified: the February 2021 update (update 3) covered 169 studies describing 232 models, almost all rated at high or unclear risk of bias, with only a couple judged promising; a later final update covered several hundred models with the same conclusion. Verify before citing. Listing: [PMC7373637](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7373637/) [S].

[U] Both are position/experience papers; neither provides a deployment-rate statistic. Wagstaff's argument that few ML results connect to real-world impact is qualitative.

## 4. Existing trackers and metadata standards to build on or harvest

### Takeaway
No existing tracker records real-world deployment status of open AI tools as a measured field. Model and repository registries (Hugging Face, GitHub, Epoch, ecosyste.ms) give cheap activity and popularity signals; curation registries (DPGA, OECD.AI catalogue) record eligibility and self-reported use cases; health scorers (OpenSSF Scorecard, Criticality Score, CHAOSS) define "maintained". The new dataset's unique contribution is a sourced "documented deployment" field joined to these harvestable signals.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Epoch AI "Data on AI Models" (Notable AI Models and related subsets): Creative Commons Attribution licence; CSV at `https://epoch.ai/data/all_ai_models.csv`; updated daily; notable subset "over two thousand" models. — [Epoch documentation](https://epoch.ai/data/notable-ai-models-documentation)
- **[V]** Epoch record fields (33): Model, Authors, Abstract, Reference, Link, Publication date, Citations, Organization, Organization categorization, Country, Domain, Task, Base model, Notability criteria, Frontier model, Possibly over 1e23 FLOP, Parameters, Training compute (FLOP), Finetune compute, Training compute cost (2023 USD), estimation method, Training hardware, Hardware quantity, Training time, Training power draw, Hardware utilization (MFU, HFU), Batch size, Epochs, Confidence, Model accessibility, Training code accessibility, notes. No field records deployment, downloads or usage; "significant use" exists only as one of five notability criteria (highly cited, large training cost, significant use, state of the art, historical significance). Notability criteria populated for 1,118 of 3,709 models. — [Epoch records documentation](https://epoch.ai/data/ai-models-documentation/records)
- **[V]** Hugging Face Hub for research: a community-maintained dataset `cfahlgren1/hub-stats` gives daily Parquet snapshots of models, datasets, Spaces, papers and related metadata; the Hub API has an OpenAPI spec at `https://huggingface.co/.well-known/openapi.json` and a Python client `huggingface_hub`. — [HF Data for Research README](https://hf-data-for-research-readme.static.hf.space/README.md)
- **[V]** Longpre et al. release a complete dataset and dashboard of HF download history (June 2020 to mid-2025, weekly, 851,000 models) with manual annotations for 2,875 head models; the dashboard URL is given inconsistently in the paper (`huggingface.co/spaces/economies-open-ai/open-model-evolution` and `huggingface.co/spaces/mmpr/open-model-evolution`). — [arXiv 2512.03073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03073v1)
- **[V]** PeaTMOSS: 281,638 PTM packages with 44,337 PTM-to-GitHub-repository links (to July 2023); SQLite via Globus; licence not stated in the paper. The only dataset found that links models to downstream code that uses them. — [arXiv 2310.03620](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03620)
- **[V]** OpenSSF Criticality Score: Apache-2.0; signals and weights are created_since (1), updated_since (-1), contributor_count (2), org_count (1), commit_frequency (1), recent_releases_count (0.5), closed_issues_count (0.5), updated_issues_count (0.5), comment_frequency (1), dependents_count (2, measured as project mentions in commit messages). Scores published as CSV/JSON in the repo's `data` directory, updated infrequently; the Google Cloud Storage/BigQuery data is being retired around August 2026. — [ossf/criticality_score](https://github.com/ossf/criticality_score)
- **[V, secondary]** CHAOSS metrics relevant to activity and abandonment (as catalogued May 2026): Code Changes Commits, Issues New/Closed, Change Requests Accepted, Time to First Response, Issue Resolution Duration, Contributors, New/Occasional/Inactive Contributors, Bot Activity, Bus Factor / Contributor Absence Factor, Elephant Factor, Release Frequency, Libyears. Critique: activity counts cannot distinguish demand from AI-generated noise; "Inactive Contributors" misses maintainers who have left auto-merge running. — [nesbitt.io](https://nesbitt.io/2026/05/27/chaoss-metrics-in-2026.html); metric list at [chaoss.community](https://chaoss.community/kbtopic/all-metrics/) [S]

### Inferences
- Deployment coverage across the verified trackers: Epoch (no), Hugging Face (downloads and Spaces usage only), PeaTMOSS (code-level reuse in public GitHub repos only), OECD catalogue (optional self-reported use cases), DPGA (not required by the Standard page fetched), Criticality Score and CHAOSS (developer activity only), FAIR4RS and MLTRL (no data). The gap the student wants to fill is real.
- The cheapest harvest path is: seed from archived Papers with Code dumps and Hugging Face paper links (paper to repo), join GitHub repository state and ecosyste.ms dependents, join HF download fields, then add human-contributed, source-cited deployment evidence.
- The OECD "removed after two years without update" rule and the DPGA annual review are precedents for a staleness policy in a community dataset.
- CC BY (Epoch) and Apache-2.0 (Criticality Score) data can be incorporated with attribution. PeaTMOSS and the OECD catalogue have no stated licence in the fetched sources, so link to them and do not redistribute.

## 5. Recommended schema fields and an operational definition of "unused"

### Takeaway
"Unused" cannot be observed directly; the defensible approach is a tiered status built from cheap public signals (repository state, inactivity, downloads, dependents) plus a separate, source-cited field for documented deployments, with an explicit "no public evidence of use" label in place of "unused". Thresholds should be borrowed from the literature (6, 12 and 24 months of inactivity; under 200 lifetime Hub downloads) and stored as raw values so they can be re-cut.

### Cited Findings (anchors for each design choice)
- **[V]** Inactivity thresholds with precedent: 6 months without commits (SciCat, with 24-month sensitivity check) — [arXiv 2504.18971](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18971); 12 months without commits (Coelho and Valente) — [arXiv 1707.02327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02327); 12 months for developer abandonment, chosen empirically over 3 to 24 months (Avelino et al.) — [arXiv 1906.08058](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08058); removal after 2 years without update (OECD catalogue policy) — [OECD.AI tools FAQ](https://oecd.ai/en/tools-faq)
- **[V]** README keyword detection of "deprecated/unmaintained" has low precision: 37% true positives on manual review. — [arXiv 1707.02327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02327)
- **[V]** Inactive does not mean failed: only 104 of 618 inactive or deprecated-flagged top projects were confirmed failures; many were complete. — [arXiv 1707.02327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02327)
- **[V]** Download threshold with precedent: Longpre et al. exclude models with fewer than 200 total downloads as negligible; HF reports about half of models fall below this. — [arXiv 2512.03073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03073v1); [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/huggingface/state-of-os-hf-spring-2026)
- **[V]** Downloads are inflated: GET and HEAD requests both count, no documented deduplication or bot filtering; CI pipelines repeatedly download models; a one-year-from-creation rolling window is one published mitigation. — [HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/models-download-stats); [arXiv 2512.03073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03073v1)
- **[V]** Use migrates to mirrors and repackagers (over 22% of recent downloads go to three intermediary accounts). — [arXiv 2512.03073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03073v1)
- **[V]** Having downstream projects is associated with lower abandonment hazard (0.78), supporting dependents as a meaningful signal. — [arXiv 2504.18971](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18971)
- **[V]** Criticality Score's "dependents_count" is only mentions in commit messages, showing how weak the standard dependents proxies are. — [ossf/criticality_score](https://github.com/ossf/criticality_score)
- **[V, secondary]** Activity counts (issues, PRs, new contributors) are increasingly inflated by AI-generated contributions and bots, so raw activity is a weaker maintenance signal in 2026 than in the studies above. — [nesbitt.io](https://nesbitt.io/2026/05/27/chaoss-metrics-in-2026.html)
- **[V]** Installability is a distinct, testable property: 28% of sampled omics tools could not be installed in 2 hours, and installability correlates with citations. — [PMC6605654](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6605654)
- **[V]** MLTRL provides an ordinal readiness scale (0 to 9, Level 9 = Deployment) usable as a "highest stage reached" field. — [PMC9585100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9585100)

### Inferences (recommendations; these are the researcher's proposals, not sourced facts)

**Proposed operational definition.** Do not assert "unused". Assign each entry one status, computed from stored raw signals at a stated snapshot date:

1. *Documented deployment*: at least one deployment record with a public source (operator, setting, date, URL) dated within the last 24 months.
2. *Signals of use, no documented deployment*: any of: one or more non-fork dependent repositories or packages; Hub downloads at or above 200 lifetime with non-zero last-30-day downloads; commits by people outside the original author group in the last 12 months.
3. *Maintained, no public evidence of use*: commit in the last 12 months, none of the above.
4. *Dormant, no public evidence of use*: archived flag set, or no commit for 12 months or more (flag 24+ months separately), and none of the use signals. This is the cheap, defensible proxy for "published but not being used".
5. *Unavailable*: repository, weights or URL no longer resolve (link rot), with Software Heritage or Wayback pointer if any.
6. *Complete/stable by declaration*: maintainers state the project is finished; excluded from the "unused" count.

**Caveats to state in the dataset's documentation.**
- Absence of evidence is not evidence of non-use: private forks, vendored copies, air-gapped and government deployments, and re-hosted weights are invisible.
- Downloads overcount (CI, bots, HEAD requests) and undercount (mirrors, caches, one download serving many users).
- Stars, likes and citations measure attention; a highly cited model can have no deployments (compare Roberts et al.: 62 published models, none usable).
- Activity metrics are gameable and, since 2025, polluted by AI-generated contributions.
- Base rates differ by artifact type; do not pool personal fine-tunes with paper-backed tools.
- Selection bias: community contributions will over-represent famous projects and English-language sources.

### Gaps
- No published, validated classifier for "unused" exists in the sources read; Castaño et al.'s two-cluster maintenance classifier and Coelho et al.'s later maintenance-level work ([ar5iv 2003.04755](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.04755) [S], not read) are the nearest.
- No source found that validates public proxies (downloads, dependents) against ground-truth deployment, so the tiering above is untested.
- A 2025 arXiv paper on predicting maintenance cessation ([arXiv 2507.21678](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21678v1) [S]) was seen but not read.
