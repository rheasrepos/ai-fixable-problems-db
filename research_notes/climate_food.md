# Open-source AI/ML tools for dangerous climate conditions and food waste/loss: released vs. actually used (as of November 2026)

Verification labels used throughout:
- **[V]** = verified in a page fetched this session (the fetch tool returns a model-generated extraction of the page, so exact wording of quotes should be re-checked before publication).
- **[S]** = seen only in a search-result title/URL/snippet, page not fetched.
- **[U]** = UNVERIFIED background knowledge (from the researcher's training data, cutoff mid-2026); not confirmed this session. These are kept out of "Cited Findings" and placed in "Gaps" or flagged in the table.

Access note: direct fetches of github.com/google-deepmind/graphcast, github.com/198808xc/Pangu-Weather, github.com/boulder-food-rescue/food-rescue-robot and huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial were blocked by the session's network allowlist, so last-commit dates and LICENSE files for those repos could not be read first-hand. Where a mirror (ecosyste.ms) or a secondary page gave the data, that is stated.

Adoption categories used (to separate what the user asked to separate):
- **A. Operational** (named agency/company runs it for real decisions)
- **B. Operational only through a derivative or the creator's own service** (the open artifact itself is not what is run)
- **C. Research/demo only** (open, maintained, no documented operational user)
- **D. Open and published but unused / unmaintained** (archived or stale, no documented user)
- **E. Deployed then discontinued**
- **F. Closed commercial, deployed** (contrast cases)

## Key Question 1 (climate): Which open AI weather models are run operationally, and what blocks the rest?

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the pattern is not "open models sit unused" but "the original open checkpoints are superseded while their descendants go operational inside rich-country centres": ECMWF runs AIFS (operational since 25 Feb 2025), NOAA runs AIGFS/AIGEFS built by fine-tuning GraphCast (Dec 2025), DWD has operationalised AICON, and the US National Hurricane Center ran Google's WeatherNext Cyclones in the 2025 season; meanwhile ECMWF switched off its real-time runs of the first-generation external models (Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, Aurora, FourCastNet) in 2026, and its own community runner `ai-models` is archived. No fetched source documents a low-income-country national met service running any of these models operationally; the only African evidence found is a regional-centre (ICPAC) experiment whose abstract names GPU cost and data access as the limiting factors.

### Cited Findings
- **AIFS (ECMWF), category A.** ECMWF took AIFS "into operations" on 25 Feb 2025, running alongside the physics-based IFS; grid spacing ~28 km vs 9 km for IFS; ECMWF claims ~1,000x lower energy per forecast and up to 20% better tropical-cyclone tracks; described as "the first fully operational weather prediction open model using machine learning" with 24/7 support. The page does not state a weights licence. [V] — [ECMWF, "ECMWF's AI forecasts become operational"](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/29308)
- **ECMWF data licence and cost barrier (largely removed in Oct 2025).** ECMWF completed its transition to full open data under CC BY 4.0 in October 2025, a year early; it had earned roughly EUR 135 million in cumulative licensing revenue 2008-2025; >93% of previously paying organisations kept a Service Agreement in the following six months; the paper notes AIFS is open source and that users "with access to a modest set of initial conditions" can run inference themselves, shifting the constraint from data access to compute/infrastructure. WMO Members get a 50% discount on paid Service Agreements. The paper gives no statistics on low-income-country use. [V] — [arXiv 2605.21673, "From Licensing to Open Access"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21673)
- **First-generation external models at ECMWF, category E (deployed for comparison, then discontinued).** ECMWF ran Pangu-Weather (Huawei), GraphCast (Google DeepMind), Aurora (Microsoft) and FourCastNet (NVIDIA) twice daily from IFS analyses and published charts; in a blog dated 7/11 May 2026 it announced "we have decided to stop the real-time operation of the external ML models with the implementation of 50r1." Reasons given: the IFS Cycle 50r1 upgrade degraded models with a fine-tuning step (GraphCast, Aurora, AIFS v1.1), Aurora and Pangu lack precipitation, GraphCast and FourCastNet were superseded by their authors' probabilistic successors, and real-time ML forecasts are now available from DWD, ECCC and NOAA. ECMWF credits the open releases with having "enabled the community to build trust in data-driven modelling." [V] — [ECMWF AIFS blog, "Farewell to the external AI models"](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog/2026/farewell-external-ai-models)
- **`ecmwf-lab/ai-models` runner, category D.** The tool that let anyone run Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet v1/v2 and GraphCast from ECMWF open data is Apache-2.0 but the repository is archived and its README says it is "no longer actively maintained or developed" and should not be used operationally; it warns that some model weights are CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0, "which does not allow commercial use"; CPU inference of a 10-day forecast takes hours vs about a minute on a modern GPU. Archive date not shown in the extraction. [V] — [ecmwf-lab/ai-models README](https://github.com/ecmwf-lab/ai-models/blob/main/README.md)
- **Licence barrier, stated by ECMWF.** In Sept 2024 ECMWF listed Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet v1/v2, GraphCast, FuXi and Aurora as runnable from its open data and warned: "different licences are attached to each of the models, with many not permitting commercial use." [V] — [ECMWF, "Run AI models yourself from ECMWF open data"](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/29193)
- **GraphCast, category B (operational via NOAA derivative).** NOAA announced on 17 Dec 2025 an operational AI suite: AIGFS v1.0, AIGEFS (31-member) and HGEFS (62-member hybrid). NOAA "used Google DeepMind's GraphCast as the initial foundation" and fine-tuned on its own GDAS analyses. AIGFS uses up to 99.7% less compute than GFS (16-day forecast in ~40 minutes); AIGEFS uses 9% of GEFS compute. Stated weaknesses: tropical-cyclone intensity, and ensemble spread still being improved. [V] — [HPCwire/AIwire, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2025/12/17/noaa-deploys-new-generation-of-ai-driven-global-weather-models/)
- **WeatherNext (Google DeepMind successor to GraphCast/GenCast), category A/B.** A secondary summary (NYU Shanghai RITS, 10 Aug 2026, self-described as AI-drafted and staff-reviewed) reports that on 6 Aug 2026 Google released code and weights for WeatherNext Cyclones and WeatherNext 2 in `google-deepmind/weathernext` under Apache-2.0 (code) and CC BY 4.0 (other materials), noting earlier GraphCast and GenCast weights were non-commercial; that the US National Hurricane Center ran WeatherNext Cyclones operationally in the 2025 Atlantic season; that full models need a TPU or H100 GPU while "mini" checkpoints fit a free Colab runtime; and that initial conditions still come from conventional numerical analysis. [V for the secondary page; primary Nature paper DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10953-2 not fetched] — [RITS NYU Shanghai](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/google-open-sources-weathernext-cyclone-forecasts-gain-a-day/)
- **Aurora (Microsoft), category C moving toward B.** Microsoft's FAQ (last modified 10 Jul 2026) calls Aurora "an open research model under an MIT license" with checkpoints on Hugging Face; it is not described as operational anywhere; operational paths are via Microsoft's own managed services (Foundry, Planetary Computer Pro); the UK Met Office is a collaboration partner, not a stated operational user; Microsoft says "any downstream consequential use should include appropriate domain-specific validation"; pre-training took ~2.5 weeks on 32 A100 GPUs. [V] — [Microsoft Research Aurora FAQ](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/aurora-forecasting/faq/)
- **FourCastNet3 (NVIDIA), category C/B.** Model card: Apache-2.0, "ready for commercial/non-commercial use", released on NGC 18 Jul 2025; 0.25 degree, 72 input variables; a 60-day global forecast in under 4 minutes on one GPU; NVIDIA GPUs and Linux only; evaluated on 2018-2019 ERA5 only. [V] — [Hugging Face nvidia/fourcastnet3 README](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/fourcastnet3/blob/main/README.md)
- **NVIDIA Earth-2 / CorrDiff, mixed A and C.** NVIDIA's blog (AMS Annual Meeting, late Jan 2026 per the HPCwire listing dated 26 Jan 2026) names users: Israel Meteorological Service uses CorrDiff operationally up to eight times daily; Brightband runs Earth-2 Medium Range operationally; GCL uses Earth-2 operationally for PV power prediction; the US National Weather Service and The Weather Company are "evaluating"; Eni is "testing" for semi-operational downscaling; AXA uses FourCastNet for R&D; Taiwan's Central Weather Administration is listed without detail. Licences not named in the blog. This is a vendor source. [V] — [NVIDIA blog, Earth-2 open models](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-earth-2-open-models)
- **AICON (DWD) and Anemoi, category A.** ECMWF's Anemoi post (1/7 Apr 2026) says AICON was "recently operationalised by the German Meteorological Service (DWD)" and that Anemoi, "open-source by design", underpins operational AIFS. It names no non-European or low-income-country users. [V] — [ECMWF AIFS blog, Anemoi](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog/2026/anemoi-european-framework-operational-ai-weather-and-climate)
- **Low-income-country uptake: only an experimental regional example found.** An EGU 2026 abstract by ICPAC (IGAD Climate Prediction and Applications Centre, East Africa) describes running AIFS ensemble inference for sub-seasonal forecasts (SON 2025) on cheaper T4/L4 cloud GPUs with half-precision, streaming IFS initial conditions from AWS open data; it states GPU compute cost and data access limit deployment; skill shown for temperature and pressure at 2-4 weeks, precipitation evaluation "ongoing". It reports no national service operating the system. [V] — [EGU26-20505](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-20505.html)
- **Early-warning coverage context.** WMO/UNDRR Global Status of MHEWS 2025 (released 11-12 Nov 2025): 119 countries (60%) report a multi-hazard early warning system; only 43% of Small Island Developing States; LDCs have the lowest comprehensiveness scores; funding is "not always targeted or sustained for long-term operation and maintenance"; a "digital divide" limits the reach of new technologies; the press release does not mention AI. [V] — [WMO press release](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/early-warning-systems-reach-new-heights-critical-gaps-jeopardize-global-progress)

### Inferences
- The clearest "open, published, and no longer used" cases in weather are the first-generation checkpoints themselves (Pangu-Weather, original GraphCast, FourCastNet v1/v2) and the archived `ai-models` runner. Their non-use is driven mainly by supersession and input drift (a model fine-tuned on one IFS cycle degrades when the analysis system changes), plus non-commercial weight licences, rather than by quality.
- "Open" did real work even where the original artifact is no longer run: NOAA's operational AIGFS exists because GraphCast's code and weights could be fine-tuned. A report should distinguish "artifact unused" from "lineage unused".
- The 2024-era barrier "needs paid ECMWF real-time data" is mostly obsolete after October 2025 (CC BY 4.0 open data); the remaining documented barriers are GPU access, staff capacity, validation for local hazards (precipitation, cyclone intensity), and sustained funding for operations and maintenance.
- All operational adopters found are high-income or well-resourced: ECMWF, NOAA/NHC, DWD, Israel Meteorological Service, plus private firms. Adoption by low-income NMHSs appears to happen by consuming ECMWF/NOAA output, not by running models; this is an inference from absence of evidence, not a documented fact.

### Gaps
- **Licences not read first-hand (blocked repos).** [U] GraphCast/GenCast code Apache-2.0 with weights CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (partly corroborated by the `ai-models` README and the RITS article, which say some/earlier DeepMind weights are non-commercial); Pangu-Weather weights BY-NC-SA 4.0 with commercial use prohibited; AIFS weights CC BY 4.0 on Hugging Face; NeuralGCM code Apache-2.0 with weights under a Creative Commons licence (NC status not confirmed); ClimaX MIT. None of these was confirmed this session.
- **FuXi, FengWu, NeuralGCM, ClimaX, Prithvi WxC, GenCast:** no deployment evidence was searched successfully. Not found: any national met service running FuXi, FengWu, NeuralGCM, ClimaX or Prithvi WxC operationally. [U] The China Meteorological Administration is reported to use Chinese AI models (FuXi/FengWu/Fengqing family) in its forecasting workflow; not verified here.
- MET Norway "Bris", ECCC and other national AI models are referenced only indirectly (ECMWF says DWD, ECCC and NOAA provide real-time ML forecasts); details not fetched.
- No WMO/ECMWF document was found giving a count of NMHSs in low-income countries that run AI weather models. The full Global Status of MHEWS 2025 report and the Early Warnings for All dashboards were not read beyond the press release.
- Exact date on which IFS Cycle 50r1 went live (and therefore the external models stopped) is not in the fetched blog.

## Key Question 2 (climate): Open flood, wildfire, damage-assessment and air-quality tools: which were published or won competitions but were not adopted by responders?

### Takeaway
The best-known "competition model" (xView2) is a counterexample: it has documented field use (Turkey 2023, California and Australian wildfires, Nepal floods), limited by imagery latency and responder trust rather than by non-use. The stronger candidates for "open, published, no documented operational user" are the NASA/IBM Prithvi-EO flood and burn-scar fine-tunes (NASA's own 2026 write-up lists only demonstrations), Google's flood-forecasting framework outside Google's own Flood Hub (open-sourced only in June 2026, one named tester), and ML wildfire-spread surrogates (still research; the operational open wildfire model, ELMFIRE, is physics-based).

### Cited Findings
- **xView2 building-damage models, category A (with limits).** MIT Technology Review (20 Feb 2023): at least two UN INSARAG search-and-rescue teams used xView2 in Adiyaman after the Turkey-Syria earthquake; co-lead Ritwik Gupta named Turkey's AFAD, the World Bank, IFRC and WFP as users; earlier use by the California National Guard and Australia's Geospatial-Intelligence Organisation for wildfires and in Nepal flood recovery. Barriers named: needs daytime cloud-free satellite imagery (first usable Turkey imagery came 9 Feb, three days after the quake), sparse imagery over poorer or remote areas such as parts of Syria, 85-90% accuracy at best, cannot see damage on building sides, and responders who are "very traditional" and slow to trust AI. [V] — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/02/20/1068824/ai-actually-helpful-disaster-response-turkey-syria-earthquake/amp/)
- A Berkeley PEER slide deck by Gupta (25 Aug 2023) confirms "xView 2 used to assess building damage over Kahramanmaras" and automated inference over Mariupol in ~5 minutes, lists the Joint Artificial Intelligence Center as partner, and reports no accuracy or failure analysis for Turkey. [V] — [PEER, xView2 Turkey slides](https://peer.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/xview2-turkey-peer.pdf)
- **Prithvi-EO flood (Sen1Floods11) and burn-scar (HLS) fine-tunes, category C.** NASA's own applications article (published 17 Mar 2026, updated 12 Aug 2026) describes flood mapping after Hurricane Helene and burn-scar mapping of the Gifford Fire (Aug 2025) as examples; every named collaborator is a university or developer (ASU, Boston University, Clark, Oregon State, Virginia Tech, UC Berkeley; NASA IMPACT, IBM Research, Julich); disaster response teams are mentioned as potential users; no operational deployment is documented; burn-scar training data covers 2018-2021 only. [V] — [NASA Science Data blog, Prithvi applications](https://science.data.nasa.gov/learn/blog/prithvi-geospatial-model-applications)
- Esri repackaged the Prithvi burn-scar and flood fine-tunes as pretrained ArcGIS models (ArcUser, 10 Feb 2025) alongside a drone damage-assessment model trained on LADI v2 (10,000+ Civil Air Patrol-labelled images); the article names no agency that has used them in a response. [V] — [Esri ArcUser](https://www.esri.com/about/newsroom/arcuser/pretrained-geospatial-ai-models-can-streamline-disaster-response)
- **Google flood forecasting, category B then newly open.** Google open-sourced its hydrology framework on 3 Jun 2026 under Apache-2.0 at github.com/google-research/flood-forecasting (PyTorch LSTM models: the 2024 benchmark model and the v2 model that "currently powers real-time global flood forecasting" on Flood Hub). Training uses the external Caravan dataset; the post does not mention release of trained weights. The only named external tester is the Czech Hydrometeorological Institute, which found forecasts "comparable in quality to traditional, locally calibrated conceptual models" and built an adapter to Delft-FEWS. WMO's hydrological forecasting chief welcomed the release; the post does not say WMO or any other agency uses it operationally. Google's own benchmark: reliable horizon extended six days in gauged basins but one day in ungauged basins. [V] — [Google Research blog](https://www.research.google/blog/the-next-chapter-in-flood-resilience-open-sourcing-googles-hydrology-framework/)
- **Microsoft AI for Good HASTE (building damage toolkit), category B/C.** Documentation describes a tool where users load satellite imagery, label a small portion, and the model predicts damage across the area; requires imagery, building footprints for one workflow, hand labels and GPU training; the docs name no user organisation and no disaster deployments (only a Spokane demo); licence not stated on the page; copyright 2026 (so maintained). [V] — [Microsoft HASTE docs](https://microsoft.github.io/haste/usage/overview.html)
- Search results (not fetched) indicate Microsoft's lab itself produced damage maps for the 2023 Maui wildfire, the 2023 Rolling Fork tornado (arXiv 2306.12589) and the 2025 Myanmar earthquake, i.e. use by the creator's own team. [S] — [arXiv 2306.12589](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12589v1); [eWeek, Myanmar earthquake](https://www.eweek.com/news/microsoft-ai-myanmar-earthquake/)
- **Wildfire spread.** ELMFIRE (open, EPL-2.0, last push to that repo listing 10 Apr 2024, 19 stars) is physics-based, and its README says that through the Pyrecast project it forecasts "most large fires in the Continental US" (category A, but not ML). [V via ecosyste.ms mirror] — [ecosyste.ms, lautenberger/elmfire](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Flautenberger%2Felmfire)
- The USDA Forest Service/Google Research deep-learning fire-spread surrogate (project years 2021-2026, page updated 4 Mar 2026) is research only; the page states physics-based wildfire modelling "has not yet become operational for wildfire management"; the DL surrogate underestimates very high spread rates, was trained with winds only up to 5 m/s, and no code repository or licence is named (training data to be opened "in the future"). Category C, and partly "published but closed". [V] — [USDA Forest Service Fire Lab](https://research.fs.usda.gov/firelab/projects/deeplearning)
- **Air quality (OpenAQ).** OpenAQ's biennial assessment (reported 13 Dec 2024): 36% of countries have no government air-quality monitoring, 9% monitor but do not publish, 55% publish and only 27% do so in a fully open format; nearly one billion people live in countries without monitoring, 90% of them in low or lower-middle-income countries; monitoring expanded only 3% since 2022. The binding constraint is absence of input data, not absence of an open platform. [V] — [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/third-of-nations-collect-no-air-quality-data-masking-health-risks-for-one-billion-people/)
- Search results indicate the US State Department halted embassy air-quality data sharing in 2025, removing a data source for many cities, and that OpenAQ publishes a 2026 platform roadmap (i.e. is active). [S; CREA page returned only a bot-check screen] — [CREA](https://energyandcleanair.org/us-embassy-aq-data-halt/); [OpenAQ roadmap](https://openaq.org/platform/roadmap/)

### Inferences
- The assumption that "xView2 won a competition and was never used" is wrong on the evidence; the accurate framing is "used ad hoc by a volunteer-led team, without an institutional owner, and limited by imagery timing and trust". No fetched source shows an agency that has adopted xView2 as a standing operational system with its own staff.
- Prithvi-EO flood and burn-scar fine-tunes are the cleanest climate example of the user's target category: openly published weights, redistributed by NASA, IBM and Esri, active maintenance, and (after more than two years of availability) no named operational responder in the developers' own showcase article.
- Benchmarks (ClimateBench, WeatherBench 2, ClimateLearn, sen1floods11, Caravan) are by design research infrastructure; "unused operationally" is not a failure for them and they should be reported as "used in research only".
- Across flood, damage and air quality, the recurring barrier is upstream data (gauges, cloud-free imagery within hours, ground monitors) and an institutional owner, not model availability.

### Gaps
- UNOSAT and Copernicus EMS use of AI in rapid mapping: not researched; no source found this session.
- No independent, quantitative post-event evaluation of xView2 accuracy in Turkey 2023 was found (the PEER deck has none).
- ClimateBench, WeatherBench 2, ClimateLearn, sen1floods11, Caravan/GRDC, xBD: licences, last-commit dates and any operational users were not fetched. [U] xBD is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial); Caravan is distributed on Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0 with per-source licences; GRDC discharge data has its own non-commercial data policy; WeatherBench 2 code is Apache-2.0. All unverified here.
- Hugging Face licence, download counts and last-update dates for Prithvi-EO fine-tunes could not be read (blocked). [U] Prithvi models are Apache-2.0.
- Which national hydromet services use Flood Hub forecasts in official warnings (India CWC, Bangladesh BWDB were early partners per [U] background) was not verified.
- ML wildfire-spread models with open code (e.g. Google's FireBench/"Next Day Wildfire Spread" dataset) and their uptake were not researched.

## Key Question 3 (climate): Quantified evidence of the research-to-operations gap

### Takeaway
I found qualitative and survey evidence that the gap exists and is organisational (trust, training, differing priorities, sustained O&M funding), but no fetched source gives a hard number such as "X% of published ML weather or disaster tools reach operations". That absence is itself a finding the report should state.

### Cited Findings
- A peer-reviewed survey of participants in NOAA's 2021 Hazardous Weather Testbed Spring Forecasting Experiment (Weather and Forecasting, vol. 40, no. 3, pp. 393-410) starts from the observation that operational forecasters appear hesitant to adopt ML products in the research-to-operations pipeline; it found participants rated ML products about the same as other guidance, but forecasters, researchers and academics differed on which factors matter most for operational success; it recommends explainable product behaviour and end-user training, and closer research-operations collaboration. [V, abstract and significance statement only] — [NSF PAR record 10596324](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10596324-assessment-how-domain-experts-evaluate-machine-learning-operational-meteorology)
- Responder trust as a barrier is stated directly by the xView2 lead: getting responders to trust an AI tool has been difficult because they are "very traditional" (Feb 2023). [V] — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/02/20/1068824/ai-actually-helpful-disaster-response-turkey-syria-earthquake/amp/)
- WMO/UNDRR 2025: early-warning funding is growing but "not always targeted or sustained for long-term operation and maintenance", and fewer than a third of countries report risk-knowledge capabilities. [V] — [WMO press release](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/early-warning-systems-reach-new-heights-critical-gaps-jeopardize-global-progress)
- ECMWF's own experience shows the maintenance cost of keeping third-party open models in a real-time suite: an upgrade to the analysis system degraded fine-tuned external models and prompted "a discussion at ECMWF about the current added value of these first-generation ML models". [V] — [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog/2026/farewell-external-ai-models)
- Compute is far smaller than for physics models once trained (AIGFS ~0.3% of GFS compute; AIFS ~1,000x less energy), so inference cost is not the main barrier for rich-country centres. [V] — [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2025/12/17/noaa-deploys-new-generation-of-ai-driven-global-weather-models/); [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/29308)

### Inferences
- The time from open publication to operations in AI weather was short where a well-funded operator existed: GraphCast (published 2023 [U]) to NOAA AIGFS (Dec 2025) is roughly two years; AIFS went from experimental to operational within about 18 months [U for start date]. The "valley of death" is therefore better described as a gap in who can cross it, not a universal delay.
- No evidence was found of an equivalent crossing for open disaster-response computer-vision models into a standing agency system.

### Gaps
- No quantified R2O statistic was found. NOAA Science Advisory Board R2O reports, the US "Weather Act" transition metrics, WMO reports on AI for NMHSs, and Climate Change AI grant-outcome reports were not located or read this session. [S] An EGU 2026 abstract (EGU26-4103) appeared in the search results for this query but was not fetched.
- The phrase "valley of death" was not confirmed in any fetched source.

## Key Question 4 (food): Which open tools and datasets for food waste and loss are actually used, and which sit unused?

### Takeaway
In food, open artifacts split sharply: open datasets and estimates are used by institutions (APHLIS feeds FAO's Food Loss Index; ReFED's open outputs are the standard US reference), while open software is mostly abandoned or single-organisation (freeCodeCamp's Pantry-for-Good was archived in July 2020 with 401 stars and no named food-bank user; Boulder Food Rescue's Food Rescue Robot was last pushed in December 2021). Open models for demand forecasting and plate-waste recognition exist as papers and benchmarks, with no documented retailer or kitchen deployment found, while deployment is concentrated in closed commercial products (Afresh, Shelf Engine/Crisp, Winnow, Leanpath).

### Cited Findings
- **Pantry-for-Good (freeCodeCamp), category D.** "An open source food bank logistics and inventory management tool"; repository "archived by the owner on Jul 17, 2020" and read-only; 401 stars, 181 forks, 660 commits; names no food bank that used it; licence file present but type not shown in the extraction. [V] — [GitHub freeCodeCamp/pantry-for-good](https://github.com/freeCodeCamp/pantry-for-good)
- **Food Rescue Robot (Boulder Food Rescue), category A (small) then D.** Rails app "for managing 'just in time' food rescue, developed by/for Boulder Food Rescue"; created 5 Apr 2013; last pushed 27 Dec 2021; 49 stars, 56 forks; permissive Beerware-style licence. [V via ecosyste.ms mirror; GitHub page itself was blocked] — [ecosyste.ms, boulder-food-rescue/food-rescue-robot](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fboulder-food-rescue%2Ffood-rescue-robot)
- **Sharing Excess app, category B (open, used by its creator only).** Sharing Excess (Philadelphia) moved from Google Forms to its own app built from 2020 and says it is "committed now and forever to sharing our software as an entirely open source project"; the post documents no other organisation deploying it; licence and repo URL not stated. [V] — [Sharing Excess on Medium](https://medium.com/sharing-excess/we-built-an-app-to-help-rescue-food-heres-why-5377e0155167)
- **Food-rescue platform landscape (ReFED, 18 Jul 2022).** Named platforms: MealConnect (Feeding America), Replate, Food Rescue Hero (412 Food Rescue, available to other organisations "through licensing"), Goodr (for-profit, $8M Series A), Take Care (Bitwise, on Salesforce), Plentiful (City Harvest), and Sharing Excess (the only one described as open source). Barriers ReFED names: organisation-specific tools may not fit others, funding is hard and building tech distracts from core operations, duplication, and "many hunger relief organizations are still quite low-tech"; "technology is not a silver bullet". ReFED projects donation coordination and matching could divert 144,000 tons per year (a model estimate, not measured adoption). [V] — [ReFED, tech-enabled food rescue](https://refed.org/articles/tech-enabled-food-rescue/)
- **APHLIS (African Postharvest Losses Information System), category A as data.** APHLIS (article dated 6 Mar 2019): FAO, custodian of the SDG food-loss indicator, uses APHLIS estimates in its Global Food Loss Index where direct measurements are missing; the African Union's 2018 Biennial Review found only five of 54 AU countries had provided adequate postharvest-loss data; Malawi, Rwanda and Uganda collected baseline data with APHLIS help. [V, self-reported by APHLIS, 2019] — [APHLIS news](https://aphlis.net/en/news/30)
- **ReFED Insights Engine, open outputs / partly closed inputs.** ReFED's methodology overview says the Food Waste Monitor draws on "more than 50 public and proprietary datasets", uses confidential data only where it offers "significant advantages over publicly available data", and publishes raw data and documentation "as much as legally possible"; updates planned one to two times a year; no licence stated on that page. [V] — [ReFED Insights Engine docs](https://docs.refed.org/methodologies/food_waste_monitor/overview.html)
- **PlantVillage Nuru (crop disease app built on open-data lineage), category A in projects, with documented drop-off.** A 2024 Frontiers in Agronomy study of cassava farmers in Cote d'Ivoire (app introduced under a Jan 2020 to Jun 2021 project; survey Jun-Aug 2023): adoption rate 45%, and 56% of beneficiaries had stopped using the app; use declined after extension officers and project support ended; barriers were smartphone unavailability (65%), perceived complexity (41%), installation failures mostly from poor internet (33%), phone loss or phones withdrawn by the project (11%); the study measured farmer perception, not diagnostic accuracy. The extraction flags internal inconsistencies between the abstract and results in that paper. [V] — [Frontiers in Agronomy 2024](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **ESA WorldCereal, category C moving toward A.** NASA Harvest (post dated 16 March, year inferred as 2026) describes WorldCereal as an "open source, cloud-based platform" for custom crop maps (with the Presto model); a November 2025 CIMMYT-hosted workshop in Zambia trained 19 participants from Zambia, Zimbabwe and Malawi including national statistics offices; the article does not confirm any agency running it operationally; limits: performance depends on local reference samples, and mixed cropping and small fields are hard at 10 m. [V] — [NASA Harvest](https://www.nasaharvest.org/news/nasa-harvest-and-esa-worldcereal-collaborate-on-custom-crop-mapping-system)
- **M5 forecasting benchmark (Walmart data), category C.** Petropoulos, Grushka-Cockayne, Siemsen and Spiliotis ("Wielding Occam's razor", arXiv 2102.13209v4): the M5 Accuracy winner (gradient-boosted trees) beat exponential smoothing by 22.4% on the official multi-level measure, but only 3.4% at the SKU-store level where ordering decisions are made; in the authors' replication on 30,490 SKU-store series, exponential smoothing was slightly more accurate than a LightGBM ensemble (RMSSE 0.713-0.714 vs 0.717) and 31-41% cheaper; bottom-up exponential smoothing beat 92.5% of 5,507 teams; they estimate ~$9,850 vs ~$5,800 per forecast cycle at Walmart scale. Caveat: the ensemble is their replication, not the original submission. [V] — [arXiv 2102.13209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.13209)
- **Open plate-waste vision models, category C/D.** Example: "Food Waste Detection in Canteen Plates Using YOLOv11" (Applied Sciences 15(13):7137, 25 Jun 2025): 175 original plate photos (860 after augmentation), open code and weights on GitHub (Xurape/Food-Waste-Detection-using-YOLOv11) and Roboflow; mAP 0.343, recall 0.322; tested only in a controlled canteen with 50 post-meal images; deployment on a Raspberry Pi in a real school is listed as future work. [V] — [MDPI Applied Sciences](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/13/7137)
- **Closed commercial contrast, category F.** Afresh (closed) claims over 200 million pounds of food loss prevented on a projected annual basis as of March 2026 and $2.7M projected annual savings in one value-grocer pilot (vendor claims reported by The Packer, 15 Jun 2026). [V] — [The Packer](https://www.thepacker.com/news/sustainability/ai-cuts-food-waste-produce-aisle-grocers-battle-cultural-overrides)
- Shelf Engine (closed; founded 2016, more than $60M raised, layoffs in 2022, "over 7,000 U.S. stores") was acquired by retail-data company Crisp for undisclosed terms. [V for GeekWire content; acquisition date 27 Mar 2025 is from the Business Wire URL/title only, S] — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/seattle-startup-shelf-engine-acquired-by-retail-data-company-crisp/); [Business Wire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20250327362637/en/Crisp-Acquires-Shelf-Engine-AI-Demand-Planning-Solution-to-Support-Optimal-Retail-Forecasting-and-Inventory-Management)
- US scale context (ReFED figures as reported by The Packer): US retail generated 3.98 million tons of surplus food in 2024, about 5.7% of the total, valued at $26.9 billion. [V] — [The Packer](https://www.thepacker.com/news/sustainability/ai-cuts-food-waste-produce-aisle-grocers-battle-cultural-overrides)

### Inferences
- The food domain has clearer examples of the user's target category than climate: Pantry-for-Good is open, was built by volunteers for a real problem, attracted hundreds of stars, and was archived with no documented adopter. Food Rescue Robot was used by its originating organisation and then went stale.
- Open data products succeed where an institution is obliged to report (SDG 12.3, Malabo Declaration, US national estimates); open software fails where it needs hosting, support and integration that small non-profits cannot supply.
- The M5 evidence suggests a different reason open forecasting models do not turn into waste reduction: at store-SKU level the accuracy gain from sophisticated open methods is small, so the value is in data integration and workflow, which is what closed vendors sell.

### Gaps
- Not researched or not found this session: FAO Food Loss and Waste Database (licence and usage), WRAP tools, open cold-chain shipment-simulation code, open perishables demand-forecasting repos other than M5, Winnow and Leanpath deployment counts, Too Good To Go, open yield models, and whether any retailer uses an open-source forecasting stack in production. [U] FAO's FLW database is free to download under FAO's CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO terms; Winnow reports deployments in thousands of commercial kitchens; Too Good To Go is a closed consumer marketplace. All unverified here.
- PlantVillage dataset: [U] roughly 54,000 lab-photographed leaf images; the original 2016 paper reported accuracy collapsing to about 31% on images taken under different conditions. Not verified this session; the search surfaced only the Nuru field-evaluation papers ([S] Mrisho et al. 2020, Frontiers in Plant Science, reports Nuru matching or beating extension agents on cassava diagnosis: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full).

## Key Question 5 (food): Documented reasons open food-waste tools are not adopted

### Takeaway
Documented reasons cluster into four groups: no organisational capacity or funding to run software (food rescue), dependence on project staff and devices (farm apps), weak real-world validation (academic vision models), and incentives and workflow rather than algorithms (retail ordering).

### Cited Findings
- Food rescue: tools built for one organisation may not fit others; funding is hard to get and building tech can be costly or distracting; many hunger-relief organisations are "still quite low-tech". [V] — [ReFED 2022](https://refed.org/articles/tech-enabled-food-rescue/)
- Volunteer-built software loses its maintainer: Pantry-for-Good archived 17 Jul 2020. [V] — [GitHub](https://github.com/freeCodeCamp/pantry-for-good)
- Farm apps: use of Nuru fell when extension officers and project support ended; 65% cited no smartphone; some phones were withdrawn by the project team. [V] — [Frontiers in Agronomy 2024](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- Retail: store managers override accurate AI order recommendations and default to over-ordering; performance metrics penalise stock-outs more than spoilage; legacy chains are not structured for the workflow changes. These barriers apply even to deployed closed tools. [V; source is a trade article quoting a vendor CEO and a ReFED/The Spoon May 2026 report] — [The Packer, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.thepacker.com/news/sustainability/ai-cuts-food-waste-produce-aisle-grocers-battle-cultural-overrides)
- Forecasting: complex open methods add computational cost for marginal or zero accuracy gain at SKU-store level. [V] — [arXiv 2102.13209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.13209)
- Academic vision models: tiny datasets (175 images), low recall (0.322), controlled-setting tests only. [V] — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/13/7137)
- Data scarcity at country level: only 5 of 54 African Union countries supplied adequate postharvest-loss data in the 2018 review. [V] — [APHLIS](https://aphlis.net/en/news/30)
- Proprietary inputs: ReFED cannot publish all underlying data because some is confidential. [V] — [ReFED docs](https://docs.refed.org/methodologies/food_waste_monitor/overview.html)

### Inferences
- "No store-level data" and "ERP/POS integration" are plausible and widely asserted barriers for open retail forecasting tools, but I found no fetched source that documents them specifically for open-source tools; the fetched evidence documents incentive and workflow barriers instead.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that surveys retailers or food banks on why they did not adopt open-source tools specifically.
- The ReFED/The Spoon May 2026 report "The Food Operating System" was not fetched.
